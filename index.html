About the workshop

01 / OVERVIEW

How do we turn increasingly capable foundation models into agents we can rely on?

AI agents connect models with tools, environments and sequences of decisions. Building useful systems raises questions beyond the capability of an individual model: how should agents act under uncertainty, recover from mistakes and demonstrate that their behaviour can be trusted?

This workshop brought together perspectives from academia and industry to explore the path from model capability to reliable systems. Three invited talks and an open panel examined where research can make lasting contributions as AI becomes more powerful.

In focus

  • Grounding, planning and tool use
  • Robustness and meaningful evaluation
  • Human oversight and collaboration
  • The future of academic AI research

New questions for AI research.

TOPICS FROM THE PANEL DISCUSSION

01What kinds of research contributions will remain valuable over time?

As stronger AI models quickly surpass today’s results, what should researchers aim for?

02What is the role of academia in the global AI race?

As frontier AI development becomes increasingly dominated by large technology companies with massive compute and data resources, where can academia make distinctive and lasting contributions?

03How should researchers make the best use of AI for research?

With AI agents and automated research systems increasingly able to search literature, generate ideas, write code, run experiments, and even draft papers, how should academics use these tools effectively? In an era of overwhelming research output, how can we identify work that is genuinely important rather than simply new?

04How should we balance research quantity and quality in the AI era?

AI is dramatically accelerating the speed of experimentation, writing, and publication. As producing papers becomes easier and faster, how should researchers balance the number of publications with depth, originality, reliability, and long-term impact?

05Should the training path for fresh PhD students change in the age of AI?

What skills should new PhD students prioritise when AI agents can already assist with reading, coding, experimentation, and writing? More importantly, how should we train them to critically evaluate AI-generated information and distinguish reliable evidence from hallucinations, misinformation, or superficially convincing claims?

Thank you to everyone who shared questions and perspectives during the discussion.
